Where should trucks and storage go for the 2026 Minnesota corn harvest?

Nick Meyer · Minnesota agriculture supply chain · Data: USDA NASS

Counties are ranked by their share of Minnesota's corn-for-grain production in past USDA NASS county estimates. Each share is then applied to a statewide planning number for this year, which gives estimated bushels, truckloads and storage per county. NASS won't publish county numbers for the 2026 crop until 2027, so past county shares are the best guide we have right now.

The statewide number defaults to the 2025 final estimate (1.70 billion bu, a record). Replace it with USDA's latest Crop Production forecast or your own figure. A semi hopper trailer carries about 1,000 bushels. Lower "share hauled" if part of the crop stays in on-farm bins.

How this works and its limits
  • Ranking. For each county: average production over the selected year(s), divided by the Minnesota total for the same years. That gives a steady "share of the state crop".
  • 2026 estimate. Share × the statewide number you enter. Truckloads = estimate × share hauled ÷ bushels per truckload. Storage = estimate × share hauled.
  • Gaps. NASS withholds some counties in some years. Those bushels are counted in the state total but can't be credited to any one county.
  • Not included. This year's weather by county, where elevators and bins already sit, and local basis. Use the ranking to decide where to look first, then adjust for those local factors.

Sources: USDA NASS Quick Stats (quickstats.nass.usda.gov); NASS Minnesota Corn County Estimates, Feb 2023 and Feb 2024; NASS 2025 State Agriculture Overview for Minnesota (2025 corn for grain: 1,696,440,000 bu).
